Soft Materials & Bioelectronics

Feng Zhang, PhD

Postdoctoral Research Fellow · NextGen Precision Health Institute
Department of Chemical & Biomedical Engineering, University of Missouri-Columbia

I build soft, tissue-integrated bioelectronic systems that sense the state of living tissue and deliver therapy in response.

My research brings together soft and porous biomaterials, wearable and implantable biosensors, and wireless drug delivery devices. The goal is closed-loop bioelectronics that sit seamlessly on or inside the body, read biochemical and physiological signals continuously, and act on them with programmable drug release and electrical stimulation, toward precision healthcare for chronic disease management and tissue regeneration.

I work with Prof. Zheng Yan in the Soft Materials and Bio-Electronics Lab at Mizzou. I received my PhD in Fiber Materials Science from Donghua University with Prof. Bin Ding, where I engineered nanoporous fibers and fibrous aerogels for molecular separation and nanofluidic ion transport. That background in pore architecture and transport is the materials foundation of my current work.

Research

Bioelectronics that integrate with tissue, read it, and respond

Four connected directions, from the materials that touch tissue to complete sensing–therapy systems.

Soft materials

Soft and porous biomaterials for seamless bio-interfaces

Tissue is soft, wet, and constantly moving, and conventional electronics are none of these. I design porous fibrous and nanofibril materials with built-in multifunctionality: they are permeable to sweat, exudate, and gas, mechanically compliant with skin and soft tissue, and able to carry sensing and therapeutic components. Current work addresses strain-resilient interfaces and selectively damping materials that suppress motion artifacts in skin-interfaced devices.

Key work: Materials Today 2025 (highlighted) · Advanced Materials 2024 · Materials Horizons 2025

Biosensing

Wearable and implantable biosensors

Diseased and healing tissues change their chemistry long before they change their appearance. I develop soft, wireless biosensors that track multiple biomarkers at once, including pH, temperature, and metabolites, directly at the tissue surface. This includes electrochemically active sensing materials and paper-based microfluidics that sample and route biofluids through the device with no pumps.

Key work: Device 2025 (invited review) · Lab on a Chip 2026 (invited review)

Therapeutics

Wireless, programmable drug delivery devices

Many therapies work best when the right dose reaches the right tissue at the right time. I engineer soft wearable and implantable platforms that release drugs on command, combining microneedles, porous drug carriers, and wirelessly powered actuation with electrical stimulation. These platforms target chronic disease management and tissue regeneration, in collaboration with Texas A&M University and the University of Nebraska Medical Center.

Key work: npj Soft Matter 2026 (invited review) · Advanced Functional Materials 2025 · Nano-Micro Letters 2026

Systems

Closed-loop, tissue-integrated bioelectronics for precision healthcare

The long-term aim is to connect the first three directions into autonomous systems. Tissue-state feedback from integrated sensors drives targeted intervention, such as on-demand drug release or electrical stimulation when biomarkers signal a change, and the device keeps adjusting as the tissue heals. This system-level work spans materials, soft and stretchable circuits, device design, and biological evaluation, with collaborators at UIC and Washington University in St. Louis.

Recent talk: Invited seminar, Saint Louis University Biomedical Engineering, 2025
